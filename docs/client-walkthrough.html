Phase 1 acceptance walkthrough

GymOS Demo Runbook

Eleven scenarios that exercise every screen in the Phase 1 build, in the order the system actually depends on. Each step says what to do and what should happen. Work through them start to finish — later scenarios rely on records the earlier ones create.

00 — Before you start

Reset to a known state

Reseeding wipes all gyms, users, sessions and financial records, then rebuilds the FitCore demo gym. Set the password yourself so every demo account starts with the same one. 

SEED_PASSWORD=DemoPass2026 npx prisma db seed
npm run dev

Then open http://localhost:3000.

Do first

Lower the minimum session duration before demoing. The default is 20 minutes, and a trainer cannot finish a session until that much time has elapsed. Sign in as the owner, go to /owner/gym-config → Policy, set minimum session duration to 1, and save.

Without this, Scenario 05 stops at "Minimum session duration (20 mins) not reached".

Note
Every seeded account is issued a temporary password, so the first sign-in for each one lands on /change-password and nothing else is reachable until a new password is set. That is the intended behaviour — pick something you will remember, per account.

01 — Configure the gym

01

Set the rate card, pay rates and policy

Owner ~8 min

Everything downstream is priced from this screen. Set it first, then watch the numbers it produces flow through bookings, payroll and the P&L. Prices set here never re-price anything already sold — that is the behaviour to look for in step 6.

  1. 1
    Sign in as owner@fitgym.com, set a new password, sign in again.
    The owner dashboard at /owner, with today's sessions and live stat tiles.
  2. 2
    Go to Gym Config → Profile. Change the gym phone number and save.
    A green confirmation bar. The change is written to the audit log — you will find it in Scenario 09.
  3. 3
    Open the Plans tab. Add a plan: Quarterly Standard, $129, 90 days.
    The plan appears in the list and becomes selectable when registering a member.
  4. 4
    Open the Session Fees tab. Set standard 50, level 2 65, introductory 0. Save.
    "PT session rates updated. Packs and sessions already sold keep their original price."
  5. 5
    Open the Pay Rates tab. Confirm L1 40%, L2 50%, off-shift premium 10%.
    These drive trainer earnings. A level 2 trainer working outside their shift earns 60% of the fee.
  6. 6
    Open Policy. Set the cancellation window to 24 hours and minimum duration to 1 minute.
    Saved. The 24-hour window decides what counts as a late cancellation in Scenario 06.
Why it matters
Raise the standard fee to $80 and re-check an existing member's pack in Scenario 07 — the pack still bills at its original per-session price. Price is snapshotted at the point of sale, so a rate change can never rewrite history.

02 — Hire a trainer

02

Create a trainer account and set their shift

Owner Trainer ~6 min

Trainers cannot self-register. The owner creates the account and hands over a one-time password that is shown exactly once and never again.

  1. 1
    As owner, go to Trainers → Add trainer. Fill in a name, email, phone; pick Level 2; set the shift to 09:00–17:00.
    A success panel showing the generated temporary password.
  2. 2
    Copy the password now. Then reload the page.
    The password is gone. It is not stored in readable form anywhere — reissuing means creating a new account.
  3. 3
    Open the new trainer from the list. Change their shift to 06:00–14:00 and approve it.
    Shift updated. Sessions booked outside these hours will be flagged off-shift and paid at the premium.
  4. 4
    In a private window, sign in as the new trainer with the temporary password.
    Forced straight to /change-password. Typing /trainer/schedule in the address bar bounces you back.
  5. 5
    Set a new password.
    Signed out and returned to login — a credential change rotates the session by design. Sign in again to reach the schedule.

03 — Register a member

03

Enrol a member and sell them a session pack

Owner ~7 min

Two different money events. A membership is recognised as revenue immediately; a session pack is not — it is money the gym owes back in sessions, and it only becomes revenue as each session is used.

  1. 1
    As owner, go to Members → Add member. Register Priya Fernando on the Quarterly Standard plan you created in Scenario 01.
    A temporary password, shown once. Copy it.
  2. 2
    Open Financials → revenue for this month.
    A new MEMBERSHIP line for $129, described as a new member registration.
  3. 3
    Go back to Priya's profile and issue a session pack: 10 sessions for $400, 90-day expiry.
    The pack appears on her profile with 10 of 10 remaining.
  4. 4
    Return to Financials and check the revenue list again.
    No $400 revenue line. That is correct — the cash arrived, but the gym has not earned it yet.
  5. 5
    Open the Deferred revenue view.
    Priya's pack listed at $400 deferred, $0 recognised, 0% utilised. Her per-session price is $40.
The rule
At any moment, deferred + recognised = what the member paid. Keep this page open through Scenarios 05 and 06 and watch the two columns trade places without the total ever changing.

04 — Request a session

04

Member requests, trainer accepts

Member Trainer ~8 min

There is no separate "hire" step. Booking is the hire mechanism — accepting a request is what makes someone your client and unlocks their profile and plans.

  1. 1
    In a private window, sign in as Priya with her temporary password and set a new one.
    Lands on /member/trainers — the member's home is the trainer directory.
  2. 2
    Pick a trainer and choose Request session. Book a time 3 days out, in person, 60 minutes. Add a goal in the notes.
    Request submitted. The form shows her remaining pack credits before she commits.
  3. 3
    Try requesting a second session with the same trainer at the same time.
    Refused — overlapping requests with one trainer are blocked.
  4. 4
    Try requesting a time in the past.
    "Cannot request a session in the past."
  5. 5
    Sign in as that trainer. Check the bell icon in the header.
    An unread notification: a new session request from Priya. Clicking it opens the schedule.
  6. 6
    On /trainer/schedule, find the pending request and Accept it.
    Status moves PENDING_CONFIRMATION → SCHEDULED. Priya gets a confirmation notification.
  7. 7
    Open Clients in the trainer's sidebar.
    Priya is now listed. Before the acceptance she was not — a pending request does not grant access to her profile.
Try this
Sign in as member3@fitgym.com (Robert Taylor), who has no session pack, and try to request a session. It is refused: "No sessions remaining — purchase a pack." A member cannot book training they have not paid for.

05 — Run the session

05

Start and complete a session, and watch the money move

Trainer Owner ~7 min

Completing a session does three things at once, and either all of them happen or none do: a pack credit is spent, revenue is recognised, and the trainer is owed their share.

  1. 1
    As the trainer, on /trainer/schedule, press Start session on Priya's booking.
    Status becomes ACTIVE and a start time is stamped.
  2. 2
    Wait past the minimum duration you set in Scenario 01, then press End session.
    Status COMPLETED. If you skipped the setup step, you get the minimum-duration refusal instead.
  3. 3
    Open Earnings in the trainer's sidebar.
    A pay line for this session. On a $40 pack session, a level 1 in-shift trainer earns $16; a level 2 trainer off-shift earns $24.
  4. 4
    As owner, reopen Financials → deferred revenue.
    Priya's pack now reads 9 remaining, $40 recognised, $360 deferred. The total is still $400.
  5. 5
    Check the revenue list for this month.
    A PT_SESSION line for $40 — priced from her pack, not from the $50 rate card.
Why $40
Priya paid $400 for 10 sessions, so each is worth $40 whatever the list price says and whichever trainer takes it. The trainer is paid a share of what the gym actually collected, not of a price the member never paid.

06 — No-shows and cancellations

06

The three ways a session ends without being trained

Trainer Member ~10 min

The distinction that matters commercially: an on-time cancellation costs the member nothing; a late one costs them the session. In both no-show and late-cancel cases the gym keeps the money but the trainer is not paid.

A — No-show

  1. 1
    As owner, book a session for Priya tomorrow via Sessions → Book session.
    Created directly as SCHEDULED — a staff booking skips the trainer's confirmation.
  2. 2
    As the trainer, press No-show on it.
    Status MISSED. Pack drops to 8. Revenue gains $40. No pay line for the trainer.

B — Late cancellation

  1. 3
    Book Priya another session, this time 2 hours from now — inside the 24-hour window.
    Scheduled.
  2. 4
    As Priya, go to My Sessions and cancel it.
    Cancelled, and flagged as late. Pack drops to 7, revenue gains $40, trainer paid nothing.

C — On-time cancellation

  1. 5
    Book one more session 5 days out, then cancel it as Priya.
    Cancelled with no deduction. Pack stays at 7 and no revenue is recognised.
  2. 6
    Reopen the deferred revenue view and add the two columns together.
    3 credits spent, 7 remaining. $120 recognised + $280 deferred = $400. The total has not moved all demo.

07 — Follow the money

07

Expenses, budgets, payroll and the P&L

Owner ~12 min

The financials screen is the reason the gym buys this. Everything on it is derived from the actions in the previous scenarios — nothing here is typed in twice.

  1. 1
    Open Financials. Review the revenue overview for the current month.
    Split across membership, PT sessions and add-ons, with the seeded records plus everything you generated.
  2. 2
    Record a manual payment: pick a member, $45, category Add-on, description "Protein & shaker".
    An ADD_ON revenue line appears immediately in the overview.
  3. 3
    Add an expense: $220, Maintenance, dated today, "Rowing machine repair".
    Listed under expenses and counted against the maintenance budget.
  4. 4
    Open Budget vs actual.
    Marketing is already over budget in the seed data — $650 spent against $500. Maintenance has moved with your new expense.
  5. 5
    Open the Payroll view for the open pay period.
    Per-trainer totals with session counts split in-shift and off-shift, including the session you completed in Scenario 05.
  6. 6
    Close the pay period, then approve it.
    Status moves OPEN → CLOSED → APPROVED. Both transitions are written to the audit log.
  7. 7
    Generate the P&L for this month.
    Revenue by category, expenses by category, and net profit. Compare it against the tiles at the top of the page.
  8. 8
    Export both CSVs: /api/export/csv?type=financials and ?type=payroll. Open them in Excel.
    Correct columns, accented characters intact, and any name beginning with = treated as text rather than a formula.

08 — Coach the client

08

Client profile, workout plan, meal plan

Trainer ~9 min

A trainer sees only the members they have actually trained. Plans are written against that relationship, so there is no way to author a plan for someone else's client.

  1. 1
    As the trainer, open Clients and then Priya's profile.
    The client view: session history, plans, and the health notes captured at registration.
  2. 2
    Create a workout plan — name it, add 4–5 exercises with sets, reps and weights.
    Saved and listed on her profile.
  3. 3
    Create a meal plan with entries across several days and meal types.
    Saved, with calories and macros per entry.
  4. 4
    Sign in as the other seeded trainer and open their client list.
    Priya is absent. Her profile and plans are not reachable from this account.
  5. 5
    Back on the first trainer's schedule, add session notes to the completed session.
    Notes saved against that session and visible on the client profile.

09 — Analytics and audit

09

Utilisation, growth, and the record of who did what

Owner ~6 min

The audit log is the compliance story: every consequential action taken in the previous eight scenarios should be sitting in it, attributed to the account that took it.

  1. 1
    Open Analytics. Read the trainer utilisation chart.
    Each trainer measured against their own shift length, so a part-timer is not scored as permanently idle.
  2. 2
    Review peak hours and the six-month member growth chart.
    Growth shows the members you registered this month. See the note under Known limits about the peak-hours figures.
  3. 3
    Open Audit Log.
    Your gym profile edit, the trainer and member you created, the pack you issued, the accepted request, the completed session, and both payroll transitions.
  4. 4
    Filter by action type — try SESSION_CANCELLED.
    The no-show and both cancellations, each recording whether a credit was deducted and how much revenue was recognised.
  5. 5
    Page through the log using the pagination controls.
    Stable ordering, newest first.

11 — Access controls

11

What happens when someone tries something they shouldn't

Owner Trainer Member ~7 min

Worth demonstrating explicitly — these are the behaviours a gym owner will be asked about when they hand accounts to staff.

  1. 1
    Signed in as a trainer, type /owner/financials into the address bar.
    Redirected to the trainer dashboard. Same for a member trying /trainer/clients.
  2. 2
    Sign out entirely and request /owner/members.
    Sent to login, with a return path so you land back where you were aiming after signing in.
  3. 3
    Try to sign in as member5@fitgym.com (Michael Brown, suspended) with the correct password.
    Refused with an account-inactive message — the password is right, the account is barred.
  4. 4
    As owner, suspend an active member while they are signed in elsewhere. Have them click around.
    Their session stops working within about five minutes. Suspension does not wait for the token to expire.
  5. 5
    Enter a wrong password six times in a row for one account.
    Throttled after five attempts, with a clear message rather than a generic failure. A correct sign-in resets the counter.
  6. 6
    Leave a session idle for 12 hours, or check the cookie expiry.
    Sessions last a working day, not a month.

Known limits in this build

Flagging these up front so nothing in the demo is mistaken for finished work.

  • Subscription & Billing is a mock-up. The plan, status and card number on /owner/subscription are placeholders generated on first view, not a real subscription. Both buttons are stubs. Treat the page as a design preview only.
  • The two policy checkboxes have no effect yet. "Deduct on no-show" and "deduct on late cancel" save, but the system deducts a credit either way. The cancellation window and minimum duration on the same tab do work.
  • Peak-hours figures read as counts, not averages, when the sample is small. With demo-sized data the chart shows the raw number of sessions in each hour.
  • "Members in Gym" on the owner dashboard counts sessions in progress, not people in the building — there is no check-in feature to derive a headcount from.
  • Receipts are placeholders. The receipt buttons on a member profile pop up a summary; real receipts arrive with the payment ledger.
  • Session times follow the server's clock. On a local machine this is correct. On a hosted deployment the server runs on UTC, which shifts displayed times until the gym's timezone is configured — fix this before running the demo on a hosted URL.
  • Temporary passwords are shown once and are not recoverable. There is no password-reset email in Phase 1; an owner who loses one recreates the account.
  • No email or SMS. Notifications appear in the in-app bell only.
